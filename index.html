Group discussion notes

Workshop: build a concept in 30 minutes

Use Claude (claude.ai) as a thinking partner across four phases. After each phase, complete the matching section below.

Ideate 5 minResearch 8 minPosition 8 minPitch 9 min

Phase 1: Ideate 5 minutes

I work in [YOUR INDUSTRY] as a [YOUR ROLE]. I'm exploring small business ideas that one person could launch in 2026 using AI. Give me 5 specific ideas. For each: who is the customer, what's the offer, and why AI makes it possible now. Avoid generic ideas like 'AI consultant.'
What to do: Open Claude. Adapt the prompt to your field. Read all 5 ideas. Pick the one that excites you. Write it below in your own words, not Claude's.

Phase 2: Research 8 minutes

Here is my business idea: [paste your idea]. Act as a market analyst. Tell me: (1) Who is the most likely paying customer? (2) What 3 existing competitors do something similar? (3) What is one assumption in my idea that might be wrong? Be specific, not generic.
What to do: Be skeptical. AI sometimes invents competitor names or statistics. Pick one claim and verify it with a search before you trust it.

Phase 3: Position 8 minutes

Based on the research above, help me write a positioning statement. Use this template: For [WHO], we offer [WHAT] so they can [BENEFIT]. Unlike [COMPETITOR], we [DIFFERENCE]. Give me 3 versions to choose from. Then ask me one question that would sharpen it.
What to do: The point is not a perfect statement. It's forcing yourself to answer: Who is this for? What do they get? Why you, not someone else?

Phase 4: Pitch 9 minutes

Turn my idea + positioning into a 60-second elevator pitch with this structure: (1) The problem in one sentence. (2) My solution in one sentence. (3) Why now / why AI makes this possible. (4) Who pays and how. (5) One question I want the listener to ask. Use plain spoken English. No buzzwords.
What to do: Read the pitch out loud once. Then edit one thing that sounds like AI wrote it, not you.

Risk debate

Three real risks for AI-era entrepreneurs. Mark the one your group thinks is the most serious, and capture the strongest argument from another group.

PickRiskWhy it matters
Vendor lock-inYour business depends on one AI provider. If their price triples or they shut down your account, you're done.
No data moatAnyone with the same AI tool can replicate your service in a weekend. What stops them?
Regulatory exposureEU AI Act, U.S. orders, sector rules. Using AI in regulated fields (health, finance, hiring) brings legal risk.

Debate language: useful phrases

Defend“The most serious risk is X because…”
“In my industry specifically, this matters because…”
Challenge“I see your point, but…”
“Wouldn't [other risk] be worse in the long term?”
Concede“You're right that…”
“I hadn't considered…”

Reminders before Week 8

Appendix: quick prompt library

Copy and adapt these for your own work. Every prompt becomes more useful when you replace the bracketed parts with real details from your industry.

1. Brainstorm in a specific industry
I work in [INDUSTRY] as a [ROLE]. Give me 5 specific small-business ideas a single person could launch in 2026 using AI. For each: customer, offer, why AI makes it possible. Avoid generic ideas.
What to do: Use this when you want range. Don't ask Claude to choose for you. Pick the one that fits your real situation.
2. Stress-test an idea
Here is my idea: [idea]. Act as a skeptical investor. Give me the 5 strongest reasons this would fail. For each, explain the underlying assumption I'm making.
What to do: Best used right after you fall in love with an idea. The job here is to find weaknesses, not validation.
3. Map the competition
Idea: [idea]. List 5 existing companies, products, or services that do something similar. For each: what they do well, what they don't, and the gap they leave that I could fill.
What to do: Verify every name Claude gives you with a real search. It will invent companies that don't exist.
4. Write a positioning statement
Help me write a positioning statement using this template: For [WHO], we offer [WHAT] so they can [BENEFIT]. Unlike [COMPETITOR], we [DIFFERENCE]. Give 3 versions. Then ask one question that would sharpen it.
What to do: Don't pick the prettiest version. Pick the one that is most specific about who and why you.
5. Build a 60-second pitch
Turn this idea into a 60-second elevator pitch: [paste idea + positioning]. Structure: problem (1 sentence), solution (1 sentence), why now, who pays, one question for the listener. Plain spoken English. No buzzwords.
What to do: Always read it out loud. If a sentence is hard to say, it's a bad sentence.
